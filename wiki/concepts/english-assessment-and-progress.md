# English Assessment and Progress

## Definition
Measuring English separately in reading, listening, speaking and grammar: a baseline at the start (step 1) and a progress test at the end of each cycle (step 20).

## Mental Model
The four skills are separate: you may read at B1 and speak at A2. A baseline lets you compare yourself with your earlier self, not with native speakers.

## Example
```markdown
# English baseline (YYYY-MM-DD)
Reading: A2/B1   Listening: ...   Speaking: ...   Grammar: ...
Unknown words per 100 (reading): ...
Typical mistakes: ...
Goal: ...
```
The CEFR scale: A1 beginner, A2 elementary, B1 intermediate, B2 upper-intermediate, C1-C2 advanced.

## Guided Practice
- Baseline (about 40 min, once): read an article and count unknown words per 100 (under 5 suits you); listen to 2 minutes without subtitles and write 3 sentences; record 1 minute of speaking; write 10 sentences (3 present, 3 past, 2 questions, 2 negatives). Save the recording.
- Progress test: record 3 minutes, read a new text, summarize it in English, compare with the baseline, fill a scorecard (fluency, accuracy, pronunciation, unknown words per 100, mistakes per 5 sentences, % understood), then plan next week from the top 3 weak points.

## Common Mistakes for Hebrew Speakers
- Judging the level by reading only; Hebrew speakers often read better than they speak.
- Comparing with native speakers instead of the earlier self; testing only comfortable skills.
