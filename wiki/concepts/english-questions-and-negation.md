# English Questions and Negation

## Definition
Forming questions with `do/does/did` and wh-words (step 11) and negatives with `don't`, `doesn't`, `didn't`, `isn't` (step 12).

## Mental Model
After `do/does/did` the main verb stays in the base form: `Did you go?`, `She doesn't work`. Wh-question order: question word + auxiliary + subject + verb. With `to be`, swap the order: `Is she here?`

## Example
| Situation | Negative | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present Simple (I/you/we/they) | don't + verb | `I don't like tea.` |
| Present Simple (he/she/it) | doesn't + verb | `She doesn't work here.` |
| Past Simple | didn't + verb | `They didn't call.` |
| to be | isn't / aren't | `He isn't ready.` |

Questions: `Where do you live?` `Why did he leave?` `What does this mean?`

## Guided Practice
- Turn statements into yes/no questions; build a wh-question for each part of a sentence; write 8 interview questions for a new colleague; contradict statements with the right negative.

## Common Mistakes for Hebrew Speakers
- Questions by raising the voice only (`You live here?`); missing auxiliary (`Where you live?`); double marking (`Did you went?`, `She doesn't works`); `What means...?`; double negatives (`I don't know nothing`).
