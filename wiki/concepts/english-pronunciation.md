# English Pronunciation

## Definition
Hearing a word, repeating it aloud, recording yourself and comparing with the original, with attention to word stress and sentence rhythm.

## Mental Model
Wrong stress hurts understanding more than a wrong letter sound. Important words (nouns, verbs, adjectives) are strong; small words (`to`, `of`, `the`) are short and weak. Do not pronounce a word as it is spelled; check the audio.

## Example
Method: listen, repeat aloud 3 times, record, compare, note one difference per word. Shadowing: repeat a 10-second sentence right after the speaker 5 times.

| Sound | Example | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| /θ/ /ð/ | think, this | tongue tip between the teeth |
| /w/ vs /v/ | west / vest | /w/ with rounded lips |
| /r/ | red | tongue does not touch the mouth |
| long vs short vowels | ship / sheep | hold the long one longer |
| final consonants | bed, bag | say the last sound |

## Guided Practice
- Daily 10 min: 5 words from today's vocabulary, minimal pairs (`ship/sheep`, `think/sink`, `very/berry`, `light/right`), one sound pair per day.

## Common Mistakes for Hebrew Speakers
- Wrong stress syllable; every word equally strong; dropped final consonants.
