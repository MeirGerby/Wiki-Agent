# English Vocabulary and Phrases

## Definition
Learning 10 useful words a day by everyday topic (home, work, food, time, transport), then learning whole phrases and collocations rather than single words.

## Mental Model
A word is more than a translation: learn its part of speech, an example sentence of your own, and the words it goes with. Chunks such as `I would like...` are learned and said as one unit.

## Example
- Frames: `I would like...`, `Could you...?`, `Can I have...?`, `How about...?`, `I'm looking for...`, `It depends on...`
- Collocations: `make a decision`, `do homework`, `take a break`, `have a look`.

## Guided Practice
- Daily: choose 10 words from one topic, write your own sentence for each, say them aloud, make cards, and review yesterday's 10 without looking. Forgotten words return the next day.
- Phrases: learn 5 with their situation (cafe, work, travel), swap the end of a frame to say something new, write a 6-line dialogue, and say the phrase from the situation alone.

## Common Mistakes for Hebrew Speakers
- Learning only the Hebrew translation; false friends such as `actually` (in fact) and `sympathetic` (caring).
- Look-alikes: `make` vs `do`, `say` vs `tell`. Word-for-word requests like `give me coffee` sound rude; use `Can I have...?`.
