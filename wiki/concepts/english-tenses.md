# English Tenses

## Definition
Present Simple, Present Continuous and Past Simple first (step 5), then Future, Present Perfect and Past Continuous as needed (step 16).

## Mental Model
Choose the tense from the time word and the meaning: habit (Present Simple), now (Present Continuous), finished (Past Simple), a link to now (Present Perfect), in progress in the past (Past Continuous).

## Example
| Tense | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Present Simple | verb (+s) | `She works in Tel Aviv.` |
| Present Continuous | am/is/are + -ing | `I am reading now.` |
| Past Simple | -ed or irregular | `We visited Haifa. I saw a movie.` |
| Future | will / going to | `I'll call you. We're going to travel.` |
| Present Perfect | have/has + participle | `I have lived here for 5 years.` |
| Past Continuous | was/were + -ing | `I was cooking when she called.` |

Learn the top irregular pasts first: go-went, have-had, do-did, see-saw, make-made, take-took, come-came, get-got, eat-ate, buy-bought.

## Guided Practice
- Write the same event in three tenses with a time word each. Use `for`/`since` with Present Perfect. Pair Past Continuous with an interruption in Past Simple.
- Present Perfect does not take finished-time words like `yesterday`.

## Common Mistakes for Hebrew Speakers
- Forgetting `-s` (`She work`); Present Simple for "now"; `goed`/`buyed`; `I live here 5 years` should be `I have lived here for 5 years`; `I have seen him yesterday` should be `I saw him yesterday`.
