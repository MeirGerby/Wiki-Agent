# Step 4 — The Verb "to be"

Stage A. Goal: use `am`, `is`, `are` correctly in positive sentences, negatives and questions.

## Learn

| Subject | Positive | Negative | Question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | I am | I am not (I'm not) | Am I...? |
| he / she / it | is | is not (isn't) | Is she...? |
| you / we / they | are | are not (aren't) | Are they...? |

- Contractions: `I'm`, `he's`, `we're`, `isn't`, `aren't`. Use them in speech.
- Short answers: `Are you a student? Yes, I am. / No, I'm not.`
- `be` also means "there is / there are": `There is a bus.` `There are two buses.`

## Guided exercise

1. Fill the gaps: `She ___ a teacher.` `We ___ at home.` `I ___ tired.` `It ___ cold.`
2. Turn into negatives and questions: `He is late.` -> `He isn't late.` / `Is he late?`
3. Answer aloud with short answers: `Are you hungry?` `Is it Monday?` `Are your friends here?`
4. Describe yourself in 5 sentences: name, age, job, city, mood. `I'm Dana. I'm 28. I'm a developer...`
5. Describe a picture or the room around you using `There is / There are` (5 sentences).

## What good looks like

- You never leave out the verb in the present.
- You answer `Yes/No` questions with the short form without thinking.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Hebrew has no present-tense "to be", so learners say `I happy`, `She teacher`, `They at home`. English always needs `am/is/are`.
- Using `are` with `he` or `she`.
- Answering only `Yes` or `No` in a quiet voice. Practice the whole short answer.

## Daily summary prompts

Write 8 sentences using `am`, `is`, `are` (3 positive, 3 negative, 2 questions).

## Review questions

1. Which form goes with `I`? with `she`? with `they`?
2. Make `They are friends.` a question and a negative.
3. What is the difference between `There is` and `There are`?
