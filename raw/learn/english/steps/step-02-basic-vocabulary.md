# Step 2 — Basic Vocabulary

Stage A. Goal: learn 10 useful words a day from everyday topics: home, work, food, time and transport.

## Learn

- Topics for the first weeks: home, work, food, time, transport. One topic per few days.
- A word is more than a translation: learn its part of speech, an example sentence, and common partners (`make coffee`, `take a bus`).
- Spaced review: look again after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week.
- Pick high-frequency words first (the first 1,000-2,000 words cover most daily speech).

## Guided exercise (10 min review + 10 new words)

1. Pick today's topic, for example **food**. Choose 10 words: `bread, cheese, hungry, cook, order, bill, spicy, fresh, menu, taste`.
2. For each word write: the word, a short Hebrew meaning, one English sentence of your own.
   - `I order a coffee in the morning.`
3. Say each word and its sentence aloud once.
4. Put them in a card list (paper or app). Front: the Hebrew meaning or a picture. Back: the English word.
5. Review yesterday's 10 words **without looking**; mark the ones you forgot and add them to tomorrow.
6. Week check: pick 20 random words and write a sentence for each.

## What good looks like

- You can use a word in a sentence, not only recognize it.
- Forgotten words come back in the next review until they stick.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Learning only the Hebrew translation. Many English words have several meanings (`bill`: invoice, bird beak).
- False friends: `actually` means "in fact", not "currently"; `sympathetic` means caring, not "nice".
- Confusing look-alikes: `make` vs `do`, `say` vs `tell`.

## Daily summary prompts

Write the 10 words with your own example sentence for each, in a Markdown list.

## Review questions

1. Give three partners of the word `take`.
2. Why write your own example sentence?
3. When do you review a new word?
