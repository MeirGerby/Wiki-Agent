# Step 8 — Graded Reading

Stage B. Goal: read short texts that suit your level and mark words that keep coming back.

## Learn

- **Graded readers** and simple news sites are written for a level. Start where you understand about 90-95% of the words.
- **Extensive reading**: lots of easy text, little dictionary use. **Intensive reading**: a short text, studied in detail. Use both.
- A word that appears 3 or more times in a text or across texts is worth learning.
- Read for meaning: skip a word if the sentence still makes sense.

## Guided exercise (10-15 minutes)

1. Choose a text of 150-300 words at your level.
2. **First read**: no dictionary. Write the main idea in one sentence.
3. **Second read**: underline unknown words. Circle the ones that appear more than once.
4. Guess the meaning of the circled words from context, then look them up. Write the sentence they came from.
5. Add 3-5 of them to your vocabulary list (step 2).
6. **Third read**: read it aloud once, paying attention to stress and rhythm.
7. Answer: Who? What? Where? Why? in English.

## What good looks like

- You read the full text without stopping at every word.
- Your repeated-words list grows into your active vocabulary.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Looking up every unknown word, which kills the flow.
- Translating whole sentences into Hebrew in your head.
- Reading texts that are too hard, then giving up.
- Ignoring prepositions: `depend on`, `interested in`, `good at`. Mark them as part of the phrase.

## Daily summary prompts

Write the main idea in two English sentences and list 3 repeated words.

## Review questions

1. Why mark repeated words?
2. What is the difference between extensive and intensive reading?
3. How much of a text should you understand to enjoy it?
