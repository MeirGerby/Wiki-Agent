# Step 1 — Level Assessment

Stage A (Language foundations). Goal: find your starting level in reading, listening, speaking and grammar, so you study the right material.

## Learn

- The CEFR scale: A1 (beginner), A2 (elementary), B1 (intermediate), B2 (upper-intermediate), C1-C2 (advanced).
- The four skills are separate. You may read at B1 and speak at A2. Assess each one.
- A baseline matters: you need it for the progress test in step 20.

## Guided exercise (about 40 minutes, once)

1. **Reading** (10 min): read a short news article for your guess of level. Underline every word you do not know. Under 5 unknown words per 100 means it suits you.
2. **Listening** (10 min): play a 2-minute video with no subtitles. Write 3 sentences about what you understood. Then check with subtitles.
3. **Speaking** (10 min): record yourself for 1 minute: who you are, what you do, what you did yesterday. Listen back and mark pauses and mistakes.
4. **Grammar** (10 min): write 10 sentences: 3 present, 3 past, 2 questions, 2 negatives. Mark the ones you are unsure about.
5. Fill the profile below.

```markdown
# English baseline (YYYY-MM-DD)
Reading: A2/B1/...   Listening: ...   Speaking: ...   Grammar: ...
Unknown words per 100 (reading): ...
Typical mistakes: ...
Goal (e.g. work meetings, videos, conversation): ...
```

Save the recording; you will compare it in step 20.

## What good looks like

- You have a level for each skill, not one overall level.
- You know your top 3 weak points.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Judging the level by reading only. Hebrew speakers often read better than they speak.
- Choosing material that is too hard, so most of it is guesswork.

## Daily summary prompts

Write 3 sentences: my strongest skill, my weakest skill, my main goal.

## Review questions

1. What do A1, B1 and C1 mean?
2. Why assess the four skills separately?
3. Why keep the first recording?
