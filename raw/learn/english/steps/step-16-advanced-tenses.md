# Step 16 — Advanced Tenses

Stage D (Intermediate and above). Goal: learn Future, Present Perfect and Past Continuous as you need them.

## Learn

| Tense | Form | Use | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Future with `will` | will + verb | decisions now, predictions | `I'll call you later.` |
| Future with `going to` | am/is/are going to + verb | plans, evidence | `We're going to travel in May.` |
| Present Perfect | have/has + past participle | past with a link to now, experience | `I have lived here for 5 years.` `Have you ever been to Rome?` |
| Past Continuous | was/were + verb-ing | action in progress in the past | `I was cooking when she called.` |

- Present Perfect does not use a finished-time word (`yesterday`). Use `ever, never, already, yet, since, for`.
- Past Continuous is often combined with Past Simple: the long action (`was cooking`) and the interrupting one (`called`).
- "As you need them": learn a tense when you meet a real situation that needs it.

## Guided exercise

1. **Future**: write 3 plans with `going to` and 3 quick decisions with `will`.
2. **Present Perfect**: write 5 sentences about your experience: `I have worked in...` `I have never...` Then ask a partner: `Have you ever...?`
3. **for / since**: `I have studied English for 2 years / since 2024.`
4. **Past Continuous**: describe what you were doing at 8 pm yesterday (3 sentences), then add an interruption (`when the phone rang`).
5. Compare: `I lived in Haifa.` (finished) and `I have lived in Haifa for 5 years.` (still true).

## What good looks like

- You pick the tense by what you want to say, not by a rule list.
- You use past participles correctly (`been, done, seen, written`).

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Using Present Simple for something that started in the past and continues: `I live here 5 years` -> `I have lived here for 5 years`.
- Saying `I have seen him yesterday` -> `I saw him yesterday`.
- Using `will` for plans that are already decided.

## Daily summary prompts

Write one sentence in each of the four forms about your life.

## Review questions

1. When do you use `have lived` and when `lived`?
2. What is the difference between `will` and `going to`?
3. Which two tenses do you often combine in a story?
