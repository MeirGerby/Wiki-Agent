# Step 9 — Whole Phrases

Stage B. Goal: learn ready-made phrases such as `How are you?` and `I would like...` instead of memorizing single words only.

## Learn

- Native speakers use chunks. A chunk is learned and said as one unit, so you do not build it word by word.
- Useful frames: `I would like...`, `Could you...?`, `Can I have...?`, `How about...?`, `I'm looking for...`, `It depends on...`, `I'm not sure.`
- Collocations (words that go together): `make a decision`, `do homework`, `take a break`, `have a look`.
- Learn phrases with a situation: where would I say it, and to whom?

## Guided exercise

1. Pick 5 phrases for today's situation, for example a cafe:
   - `Can I have a coffee, please?`
   - `I would like something to eat.`
   - `How much is it?`
   - `Could I pay by card?`
   - `Is there a table for two?`
2. Say each phrase aloud with natural rhythm 5 times.
3. Replace the end to make new sentences: `I would like a coffee / the bill / to book a table.`
4. Write a 6-line dialogue using all 5 phrases and read both sides aloud.
5. Cover the English and say the phrase from the situation alone (`you want the bill`).
6. Keep a phrase notebook, grouped by situation: cafe, work, travel, phone.

## What good looks like

- You say the phrase fluently, not word by word.
- You can swap parts of a frame to say something new.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Translating a Hebrew phrase word for word: `I want to get a coffee` is fine, but `give me coffee` sounds rude. Use `Can I have...?` or `I would like...`.
- Missing `please` and softeners (`could`, `would`) in requests.
- Memorizing the phrase but not the situation.

## Daily summary prompts

Write the 5 phrases with the situation for each.

## Review questions

1. What is a collocation? Give two.
2. How do you make a polite request?
3. Why learn a phrase with its situation?
