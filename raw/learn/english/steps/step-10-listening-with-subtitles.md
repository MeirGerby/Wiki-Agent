# Step 10 — Listening Comprehension with Subtitles

Stage B. Goal: watch a short video with English subtitles, listen again without subtitles, and check what improved.

## Learn

- English subtitles (not Hebrew) connect the sound to the written word.
- The three-pass method: watch **with** English subtitles, listen again **without** them, then compare how much you understood.
- Subtitles are a tool for learning, not for reading: look away and listen when you can.
- Short is better: a 2-4 minute clip, studied well, beats a long episode.

## Guided exercise (15 minutes)

1. Pick a 2-4 minute clip with English subtitles (interview, vlog, show scene).
2. **Pass 1, without subtitles**: write 3 things you understood.
3. **Pass 2, with English subtitles**: pause and write 5 phrases you did not catch. Note how they are written.
4. **Pass 3, without subtitles**: listen again and write how much more you now catch (give it a rough percentage).
5. Pick 1-2 phrases and say them aloud with the same rhythm.
6. Log progress:

```markdown
Clip: ...  Length: ...
Pass 1: understood ~ 40%
Pass 3: understood ~ 70%
Missed because: speed / linking / slang / vocabulary
Phrases: ...
```

## What good looks like

- The percentage rises between pass 1 and pass 3.
- You can name the type of difficulty (speed, linking, slang, vocabulary).

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Using Hebrew subtitles. You read and do not listen.
- Reading all subtitles on every pass.
- Missing linked sounds: `want to` -> `wanna`, `going to` -> `gonna`, `did you` -> `didja`.

## Daily summary prompts

Write the clip's gist in three English sentences and the phrases you learned.

## Review questions

1. Why English subtitles and not Hebrew ones?
2. What are the three passes?
3. What does `gonna` stand for?
