# Step 15 — Practical Conversation

Stage C. Goal: practise a 5-minute conversation: introductions, ordering food, asking for help, or a work conversation.

## Learn

- A conversation has a shape: greeting, small talk, the main purpose, closing.
- Useful moves: ask for repetition (`Sorry, could you say that again?`), check meaning (`Do you mean...?`), buy time (`Let me think...`), and close politely (`Nice to meet you.`).
- Fluency before accuracy: keep going, fix later.
- Practise with a partner, a tutor, or a role-play recorded alone.

## Guided exercise (5-10 minutes)

Pick one scenario a day:

| Scenario | Goal | Starter |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introduction | name, job, city, hobby | `Hi, I'm... Nice to meet you.` |
| Ordering food | order, ask, pay | `Can I have...? How much is it?` |
| Asking for help | directions or a problem | `Excuse me, could you help me?` |
| Work chat | status and a question | `I'm working on... Could you check...?` |

1. Prepare 5 phrases and 3 questions (use step 9 and step 11).
2. Play both roles aloud, or do it with a partner. Set a 5-minute timer.
3. Record it. Listen once: mark pauses, mistakes, and phrases you could say better.
4. Repeat the same scenario a second time and improve it. Repeating is what builds fluency.
5. Log: one thing that went well, one thing to fix.

## What good looks like

- You keep the conversation going for 5 minutes.
- You use repair phrases instead of going silent.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Answering with one word. Add a reason or detail: `Yes, I do. I like cooking on weekends.`
- Direct requests that sound rude in English: use `Could you...?` and `please`.
- Going silent when a word is missing instead of describing it.

## Daily summary prompts

Write the best sentence you said, and one you want to say better next time.

## Review questions

1. Name three phrases for when you do not understand.
2. How do you make a request polite?
3. Why repeat the same scenario twice?
