# Step 19 — Thinking in English

Stage D. Goal: phrase simple thoughts directly in English, without translating every sentence from Hebrew.

## Learn

- Translation adds delay and mistakes. Direct thinking links the idea to the English words.
- Start small: simple thoughts about the present (`I'm hungry. I need to call Dan. It's late.`).
- Use English **inside** your head for routine things: plans, lists, reactions.
- Use pictures and situations as the trigger, not a Hebrew sentence.

## Guided exercise

1. **Object labels** (2 min): look around and name 10 objects and their actions in English only. `door - open the door`.
2. **Inner commentary** (3 min): for five minutes while you walk or tidy, think only in English: what you see, what you want, what you will do.
3. **To-do list in English**: write tomorrow's list with no Hebrew at all. `Call the bank. Buy milk. Finish step 19.`
4. **Describe a situation without translating**: look at a photo for 30 seconds, then say 5 sentences about it, or write them.
5. **Retell**: after a short video or article, summarize it in 3 English sentences without writing Hebrew first.
6. When you find a gap, note the missing English word. Learn it that day.

## What good looks like

- Your first thought for simple things arrives in English.
- You use simple words instead of searching for the perfect translation.

## Common mistakes for Hebrew speakers

- Building the sentence in Hebrew, then translating word by word. This creates forms like `I have 28 years` (-> `I am 28`).
- Waiting for the perfect word. A simpler word is better than silence.
- Using the Hebrew word order for adjectives, time and negatives (see steps 3 and 12).

## Daily summary prompts

Write today's inner commentary in 5 sentences, and the words you were missing.

## Review questions

1. Why does direct thinking help fluency?
2. What do you do when you do not know the exact word?
3. Give two things you can do in English in your head every day.
